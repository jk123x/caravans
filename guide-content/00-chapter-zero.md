## What Solar Actually Does for You (the Short Version)

When you're plugged in at a caravan park, everything works. Fridge, lights, phone charger, no worries.

When you're free camping, there's no plug. Your caravan has a battery, but on its own it'll last a night, maybe two, before things start going flat.

Solar keeps that battery charged using sunlight. Panels on the roof soak up the sun during the day, and that energy goes into the battery. Your stuff keeps running. You don't need to plug in.

That's it. That's the whole point. Solar = you can camp without plugging in.

## Why People Choose Solar

**The best camping spots don't have power.** River camps, beach spots, bush camps, free stays on station properties. These are the places everyone talks about. Solar lets you actually use them for more than one night.

**Powered sites are expensive.** $40-$60+ a night adds up fast over a few weeks. Unpowered sites and free camps save you a fortune.

**You stop planning around power.** No more "can we stay here or do we need to find a park tonight?" You just... go where you want.

## What a Setup Looks Like

Every caravan solar setup has the same basic bits:

- **Panels on the roof** (and maybe a portable one you set up at camp) — these charge your battery using sunlight
- **A battery inside the caravan** — this stores the power and runs your stuff
- **A few bits in between** that manage the charging — you don't need to understand these, you just need to buy the right ones
- **A gauge** that tells you how full the battery is — like a fuel gauge

That's the whole setup. Panels charge battery, battery runs your stuff, gauge tells you how much you've got left.

## What Does It Cost?

Depends on how you travel. There are three rough levels:

**Basic weekend setup: $1,500 - $2,500.** Good enough for a couple of nights off-grid. Fridge, lights, phone charging.

**Mid-range tourer setup: $3,000 - $5,000.** This is where most people end up. Comfortable free camping for as long as you want in decent weather. Runs the fridge, TV, laptops, and phone chargers without stress.

**Full-time setup: $5,000 - $8,000+.** For people living on the road. Runs the heavy stuff too: microwave, coffee machine, hair dryer.

Some people install it themselves, others pay someone to do it ($1,000-$3,000 for labour). Either way, the first step is the same: figuring out what you need.

## The One Thing Worth Figuring Out

You don't need to learn how electricity works. The one thing worth working out is **how much power your caravan uses in a day**. That single number tells you what size system to buy.

It's really simple. Every appliance has a number on its label that tells you how much power it uses. Your fridge might say 60. Your TV might say 40. You go through what you've got and add them up. (We also have a free calculator on our website that does this for you — **[TODO: insert calculator URL]**.)

**Your fridge** is the big one. It runs 24 hours a day and uses the most power of anything in your caravan.

**Lights, TV, phone chargers** — these all use a bit, and they add up over the course of a day.

**The heavy stuff** — microwave, coffee machine, hair dryer — uses a lot, but only for a few minutes at a time.

A typical touring setup (fridge, lights, TV, phone chargers, laptop) uses about **2,000 to 2,500 watt-hours per day**. Yours might be more or less depending on what you've got.

If you want to figure out your exact number, use the worksheet on the next page. It lists every common caravan appliance with its typical power draw. You just tick what you have and add it up. Takes about 10 minutes.
