## The Whole System in Plain English

**Solar panels** sit on your roof. They turn sunlight into power. Some people also carry a folding panel they set up at camp — handy when your roof panels are in the shade.

**A battery** stores that power. This is the fuel tank. Everything runs off it.

**A charge controller** sits between the panels and the battery to make sure the battery charges safely. You buy the right size and forget about it.

**A DC-DC charger** tops up the battery from your car while you drive. This is a big deal — a few hours of driving puts a serious amount of charge back in.

**An inverter** lets you plug in normal household things (laptop charger, microwave, hair dryer). It converts the battery power to the same type your house uses.

**A battery monitor** is your fuel gauge. It tells you how much power you've got left. Don't skip this — without it you're guessing, and that's how people wear out their batteries early.

That's it. Six things. The rest of this guide tells you exactly which ones to buy and what size.

## One Quick Concept (Then We're Done)

You'll see the word **watt-hours** a lot in this guide. It's how we measure your daily power use.

It works like this: if something uses 60 watts and runs for 10 hours, that's 600 watt-hours. Your fridge uses about 60 watts and runs all day, so it uses about 1,440 watt-hours per day. Your TV uses about 40 watts and you watch it for 3 hours, so that's 120 watt-hours.

You figured out your daily watt-hours number in the Power Audit (or Chapter 0 if you did that first). That number drives everything — the battery size, the amount of solar, the whole lot. If you haven't done it yet, flip to the Power Audit Worksheet now. It takes 10 minutes.
