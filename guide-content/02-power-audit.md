## Figure Out Your Number

If you already did the Power Audit from the free Chapter 0 download, you've got your number. Skip to "Which Tier Are You?" below.

If not, here's the deal: we need to figure out roughly how much power your caravan uses in a day. This takes about 10 minutes, and it's the one piece of homework in this entire guide. Everything else flows from it.

Don't stress about being exact. Within 20% is plenty. We'll add a buffer for the stuff you forget.

## Walk-Through: Dave and Sarah's Setup

Dave and Sarah are tourers. Weeks on the road, mix of caravan parks and free camping. Here's their typical day off-grid:

**The fridge runs all day and all night.** It's by far the biggest power draw. About 60 watts, running 24 hours. That alone is about 1,440 watt-hours.

**Lights come on in the evening.** All the LEDs together are about 30 watts, on for 5 hours. About 150 watt-hours.

**TV after dinner.** 40 watts, 3 hours. About 120 watt-hours.

**Phones and laptop during the day.** Maybe 100 watts total for a few hours. About 220 watt-hours.

**Water pump, diesel heater, range hood.** These all add a bit — maybe 220 watt-hours combined.

**Dave and Sarah's daily total: about 2,150 watt-hours.**

Then they add 25% as a buffer (things always use more than you expect): **about 2,700 watt-hours per day.**

That's it. No complicated formulas. Just: what do you have, how long is it on, roughly how much does it use?

Your turn. Use the **Power Audit Worksheet** at the back — it lists every common caravan appliance with typical power draws so you don't have to look anything up.

## Which Tier Are You?

Your daily number tells you what kind of system you need. Pick the one that fits:

### Weekender — Under 1,500 watt-hours/day

**The short version:** Short trips, mostly caravan parks, some free camping. Fridge, lights, phones.

**What you'll spend on the full setup:** $1,500 - $2,500

**What you get:** A couple of nights free camping without worrying about power. Your car tops up the battery when you drive. A single roof panel keeps things going in between.

### Tourer — 1,500 to 3,000 watt-hours/day

**The short version:** Weeks on the road, mix of parks and free camping. Fridge, lights, TV, laptops, phones.

**What you'll spend on the full setup:** $3,000 - $5,000

**What you get:** Free camp as long as you want in decent weather. This is where most people end up. Comfortable, no stress, don't have to think about power much.

### Full-timer — Over 3,000 watt-hours/day

**The short version:** Living on the road or extended trips. Everything above plus microwave, coffee machine, hair dryer.

**What you'll spend on the full setup:** $5,000 - $8,000+

**What you get:** Total independence. Park anywhere with decent sun and live normally.

### Quick Note on Air Conditioning

Running air con off-grid on a standard setup isn't practical — it uses more power than everything else combined. If that's a must-have, you're looking at a specialised 48V system ($10,000+) or a generator. This guide covers standard 12V setups, which is what the vast majority of caravanners use.
