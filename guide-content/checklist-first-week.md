# Checklist: Your First Week With Solar

*Your system is in. Here's how to get familiar with it — think of it like the first few drives in a new car. After a week this will all be second nature.*

---

## Day 1: Get to Know Your Battery Monitor

Your battery monitor is like the fuel gauge in your car. It shows a percentage — 100% is full, 0% is empty.

☐ **Find it and read it.** It should show a percentage. If you're in the sun or plugged into shore power, you'll see it charging.

☐ **Turn on your usual stuff** — fridge, lights, whatever you'd normally run. Watch the monitor for a minute. You should see it showing power going out. Everything's connected and working.

☐ **Test the inverter.** Plug in your phone charger. Works? Good. Now try the biggest thing you'll run (microwave, hair dryer) — one at a time. Works? You're set.

☐ **Plug in your portable panel** via the Anderson socket. Check the monitor shows extra charging coming in. Pack it away. Now you know how that works too.

## Day 2-3: Learn the Pattern

Every setup has a daily rhythm. You'll pick it up quickly.

☐ **Check the monitor first thing in the morning** (before the sun gets going). This shows how much the overnight use — mostly the fridge — draws down the battery. Going from 100% to 85% overnight is totally normal.

☐ **Check again in the late afternoon.** Has the solar brought it back up? If you're back near 95-100%, your system is keeping up easily. If it's hovering around 80%, set up the portable panel for a few hours or be a bit more conservative with power that evening.

☐ **Notice the rhythm.** Battery drops overnight, solar fills it during the day. That's the whole cycle. As long as the top-up roughly matches the drawdown, you're golden.

## What You'll See (All Normal)

- **Battery sits between 70-100%** on a typical day — that's healthy
- **Solar output is high around midday, low morning and evening, zero at night** — that's just how sun works
- **The fridge draws power in bursts** — the compressor cycles on and off, so you'll see the power draw jump up and down
- **The inverter hums quietly** when it's switched on
- **Cloudy days produce less solar** — one or two cloudy days in a row is fine, your battery buffer handles it. If it's cloudy for days, a few hours of driving tops things up via the DC-DC charger

## If Something Seems Off

**Battery level dropping even though it's sunny.**
→ Check if something is shading the panels — a tree branch, the awning, even the A/C unit shadow at certain times of day. Move the van or set up the portable panel in full sun.

**Inverter switches off when you plug something in.**
→ You're running too much at once. Use one big appliance at a time — microwave OR hair dryer, not both.

**Battery gets low after a few days of overcast weather.**
→ Drive for a couple of hours. The DC-DC charger will put a solid charge back in. This is exactly what it's there for.

## After a Week

You'll barely think about it. A quick glance at the battery monitor in the morning becomes like checking the fuel gauge — takes two seconds, tells you everything you need to know.

If the battery is staying above 60-70% most of the time, your system is well sized and everything is working properly. Nice job.
