## Mistake 1: Buying AGM Now, Lithium Later

> **Cost: $400 - $800 straight in the bin**

The most common beginner mistake. You buy AGM batteries because they're cheaper upfront. Within a year you realise they don't hold enough charge, they're heavy, and they're slow to charge. You upgrade to lithium. The AGMs go in the bin.

**The fix:** Buy lithium from the start. Yes, it costs more. No, it's not a waste — it's cheaper over 5 years than going through two sets of AGMs.

---

## Mistake 2: Not Enough Solar for Your Battery

> **Cost: $300 - $1,000+ in shortened battery life**

A big battery with not enough solar to charge it properly. The battery sits undercharged day after day, which wears it out years earlier than it should. We see this a lot: a 200Ah lithium with only 100-150W of solar.

**The fix:** Solar should be roughly 1.5-2× your battery Ah. 200Ah battery? Get 300-400W of solar. The shopping lists in Section 4 have this balanced correctly.

---

## Mistake 3: Expecting the Number on the Solar Panel Box

> **Cost: $500 - $1,500 in panels that don't deliver what you expected**

Your "400W" solar panels produce maybe 200-280W in real conditions. Flat mounting, heat, dust, shade — it all adds up. People who don't know this buy too little solar and can't figure out why their battery never fills up.

**The fix:** We've already accounted for this in the sizing. When this guide says "get 400W," the real-world performance is baked in. Don't panic when your 400W panels show 250W on a normal day — that's expected.

---

## Mistake 4: Thin Cables

> **Cost: $200 - $500 to rewire**

12V systems run higher current than your house wiring, so they need thicker cables. Thin cables waste power and don't perform well — you end up paying someone to rewire later.

**The fix:** Your installer will size the cables correctly. If you're going DIY, go one size thicker than you think you need. Thicker cable is cheap. REDARC and Enerdrive both publish free cable sizing guides on their websites.

---

## Mistake 5: No Battery Monitor

> **Cost: $500 - $1,500 in damaged batteries**

Without a monitor, you're guessing how much charge you've got left. You end up running the battery down further than you should, which wears it out faster. It's like driving without a fuel gauge — you'll get caught short eventually.

**The fix:** Victron SmartShunt, $130-$160. It's 10% the cost of your battery. Shows your exact battery level on your phone. Not optional.

---

## Mistake 6: Modified Sine Wave Inverter

> **Cost: $200 - $500 in damaged devices + replacing the inverter**

You buy a modified sine wave inverter because it's $50-$100 cheaper. It produces rough power that doesn't play nicely with laptops and chargers. When something stops working properly, you've spent more than the $100 you saved.

**The fix:** Always buy pure sine wave. Always. It's identical to what comes out of the wall at home. Every appliance works perfectly with it.

---

## Mistake 7: No External Anderson Plug

> **Cost: $300 - $500 worth of portable panel you can't easily use**

You buy a portable panel but there's nowhere to plug it into the outside of your caravan. So you're running cables through windows, wedging doors open, or just not bothering to set it up. A $300+ panel sitting in the storage bay because it's too much hassle to connect.

**The fix:** An external Anderson plug socket costs $20-$40 and takes 30 minutes to install. It gives your portable panel a clean, weatherproof connection point. Make sure it's on your shopping list (it's included in ours).

## The Pattern

Every one of these comes from one of three places:

**Buying cheap and replacing later.** (Mistakes 1, 6) The right gear once is always cheaper than the wrong gear twice.

**Not knowing how things actually perform.** (Mistakes 2, 3) Real-world performance is different from lab specs. This guide accounts for that.

**Skipping the small stuff.** (Mistakes 4, 5, 7) Cables, monitors, and Anderson plugs are cheap. The problems they prevent are expensive.
