# Section 6: The Mistakes That Cost People Real Money

*~3 pages. Scannable list of common mistakes. Each one: what it is, what it costs, how to avoid it.*

---

If you've followed this guide and bought from the shopping lists, you've already avoided all of these. Have a read through anyway. It's good to know what you're not doing.

## Mistake 1: Buying AGM Now, Lithium Later — $400-$800 wasted

You buy AGM batteries because they're cheaper upfront. Within a year you realise they don't hold enough charge, they're heavy, and they're slow to charge. You upgrade to lithium. The AGMs go in the bin. Buy lithium from the start. It's cheaper over 5 years than going through two sets of AGMs.

## Mistake 2: Not Enough Solar for Your Battery — $300-$1,000+ in shortened battery life

A big battery with not enough solar to charge it properly. The battery sits undercharged day after day, which wears it out years earlier than it should. Solar should be roughly 1.5-2× your battery Ah. 200Ah battery? Get 300-400W of solar. The shopping lists in Section 4 have this balanced correctly.

## Mistake 3: Trusting the Number on the Solar Panel Box — $500-$1,500 in panels that underdeliver

Your "400W" solar panels produce maybe 200-280W in real conditions. Flat mounting, heat, dust, shade, it all adds up. People who don't know this buy too little solar and can't figure out why their battery never fills up. This guide's sizing already accounts for real-world performance, so you're covered.

## Mistake 4: Thin Cables — $200-$500 to rewire

12V systems run higher current than your house wiring, so they need thicker cables. Thin cables waste power and don't perform well. You end up paying someone to rewire later. Your installer will size these correctly. Going DIY? One size thicker than you think you need. Thicker cable is cheap.

## Mistake 5: No Battery Monitor — $500-$1,500 in worn-out batteries

Without a monitor, you're guessing how much charge you've got left. You run the battery down further than you should, which wears it out faster. It's like driving without a fuel gauge. A Victron SmartShunt costs $130-$160 and shows your exact battery level on your phone.

## Mistake 6: Modified Sine Wave Inverter — $200-$500 in devices that stop working properly

You save $50-$100 on a cheaper inverter that produces rough power. It doesn't play nicely with laptops and chargers. When something stops working, you've spent more than you saved. Always buy pure sine wave. It's identical to what comes out of the wall at home.

## Mistake 7: No External Anderson Plug — $300-$500 portable panel you never use

You buy a portable panel but there's nowhere to plug it into the outside of your caravan. So you're running cables through windows, wedging doors open, or just not bothering. An external Anderson plug socket costs $20-$40 and takes 30 minutes to install. Make sure it's on your shopping list (it's included in ours).

## The Pattern

Every one of these comes from one of three places:

**Buying cheap and replacing later.** (Mistakes 1, 6) The right gear once is always cheaper than the wrong gear twice.

**Not knowing how things actually perform.** (Mistakes 2, 3) Real-world performance is different from lab specs. This guide accounts for that.

**Skipping the small stuff.** (Mistakes 4, 5, 7) Cables, monitors, and Anderson plugs are cheap. The problems they prevent are expensive.
